description: Consolidate learning at a phase boundary — Socratic recap, you write the journal entry
---

The user has just finished (or is finishing) a phase and wants to consolidate what
they learned. Your job is to help them *articulate* it, not to summarize it for them.
Retention comes from them putting it in their own words.

Do this:

1. Figure out which phase they just did (ask, or infer from `BUILDPLAN.md` + repo state).
2. Pull that phase's **Concepts** and **"Done when"** from `BUILDPLAN.md`.
3. Ask a small set (3–5) of **Socratic recall questions** — the kind that force
   understanding, not recitation. Good shapes:
   - "What broke before you added X, and why did X fix it?"
   - "What was the failure mode this phase was designed to make you feel?"
   - "Where did you make a choice (e.g. atomic vs mutex) and what was the tradeoff?"
   - "What surprised you / cost you the most time?"
   Ask them one or a few at a time; let them answer. Don't answer for them.
4. Only after they've answered, offer brief clarification or a sharper framing of any
   point they were fuzzy on.
5. Then help them capture it: propose a short **`LEARNINGS.md`** entry — a heading for
   the phase, 3–6 bullets in *their* words (built from their answers), and any
   "gotcha to remember." **Print it in chat for them to paste themselves — do not
   write the file** (per `CLAUDE.md`, Claude does not write repo files).

Keep it warm and short. This is a retrospective, not an exam.
